Case study 1 of 3

Flanking a legacy incumbent at a global bank

My role: Global Account Manager (strategic AE)

Deal size
$6.4M TCV
Commitment
3 years
First meeting → PO
~8 months
Since
ARR almost 2×

1Getting the first meeting

Account research showed a legacy competitor with a large footprint. Instead of a rip-and-replace, I ran a flanking play: identified the IT stakeholders running the competitor's software and launched a cold outbound campaign (email, phone, LinkedIn) positioning UiPath capabilities they didn't have.

Discovery confirmed that UiPath's attended automation could complement the existing tech stack.

2Multithreading

From IT we threaded into the business and found the pain in customer service: setting up a payment plan with a delinquent customer took a 45-minute phone call. By bringing legacy systems together, our software could cut that in half.

3Making it big

We told Barclays from the start that we were aiming for a large, transformational deal. While we proved the customer service use case in a PoC, we built demand across other back-office functions in parallel, turning one use case into a multi-use-case plan.

4Closing

When the timing was right, I brought in UiPath senior leadership to align with Barclays executives and show how much the deal mattered. Result: a $6.4M, 3-year commitment. The account's ARR has almost doubled since.

1Getting the first meeting

Sandvik had just announced a corporate objective, the "digital shift", which included transforming back-office and admin functions with automation. Thomas and I built a multi-channel outreach campaign and mapped the stakeholders to target.

Cold email and LinkedIn outreach got Thomas meetings with the Head of Process Automation and his boss, the CIO.

2Competitive position

A competitor was already in, but with a small installed base and real problems. I coached Thomas to surface those problems fully, then position UiPath on business outcomes: lower TCO, less maintenance, and room for many more automation use cases.

3Multithreading

That opened a series of workstreams we ran in parallel: infosec, compliance, deployment, procurement and pricing, each with its own stakeholders to win.

4Making it big

We sold the future value of the platform. Sandvik bought into projected demand, not just today's use cases, and committed to 3 years at $1.5M. The account is now about double that.

Big from day one: tying automation to operating margin

Commitment

1Getting the first meeting

A meeting with a more junior stakeholder showed that Securitas had made good progress with AI use cases in its core business and wanted to do more in the back office. Jenny used that insight in cold outreach to the Global Head of AI, which got us the first meeting.

Jenny then built a strong champion and nurtured the relationship. In early summer 2025 the champion, Jenny and I drew a roadmap to a deal on a whiteboard, and we closed it that same fiscal year.

2Finding the metric

We uncovered the number the whole company was measured on: operating margin, from 8% to a 12% target. Everything we did after that was tied back to it.

3Multithreading across Europe

We ran workshops across markets, including France, Germany, Sweden and Poland, collecting use cases from the business into a large demand file. That became a compelling business case our champion took to the President of Securitas Europe.

4Closing

The President bought in and met with UiPath senior leadership. We closed a $4.2M, 3-year transformation deal in about 7 months. Consumption is on track, and the services side has grown since.

Tele2: an example of opening doors through my network

My key stakeholder at Ericsson, a customer I used to sell to, moved to Tele2. I reached out to him in his first week in the new role and kicked off a sales process for my AE.

The pattern I coach

  1. Find the pain or the critical metric. A public objective ("digital shift"), a painful process (a 45-minute call), or a company-wide KPI (8% → 12% margin).
  2. Determine the account strategy. Build the business case, flank the competition, rip-and-replace as credibility and value grows.
  3. Thread from IT into the business. The budget and urgency sit with the people who feel the pain.
  4. Make it big from day one. Signal transformational intent early and build a demand file across functions and markets.
  5. Bring in executives at the right moment. Pair the champion's business case with senior leadership alignment.

Who I hire

I've selected five AEs at UiPath. Two have reached President's Club, with a third projected to qualify this fiscal year. I assess candidates on their ability to originate opportunities, run complex sales cycles and demonstrate sustained performance.

  • Hunger, grit and ambition
  • A track record of sales performance
  • Has overcome adversity at some point in their career
  • Business acumen: can engage senior stakeholders
  • Technical curiosity: can run the first 1–2 Cognition meetings on their own

My standard screening question: "Walk me through a major new-logo deal you personally originated. How did you identify the account, get the first meeting, build the opportunity and close it?"
